## London Life



Upon arriving my father worked for London Transport briefly as a conductor and motorman (driver) of those tube trains, saving to ultimately buy his shop and our home above it in North London. I was born two years later in the summer of 1969 - that time of love and rockets. The 'tube', its ubiquitous symbols, and the trains that flew in and out the station were so impactful to my infant consciousness that before I even learned to speak, I created a word combining the sounds - bells, whistles and flourish of the warm air that I associated with them. What child doesn't love a train? I'd point and exclaim in gleeful toddler delight 'Toot-aah-pi! thinking 'Nothing stops that train!' My family still grin and point in reminiscence when we're in London and 'going underground'. 

**Your Arsenal**

England *international* Sol Campbell was born Sulzeer Jeremia in the East London borough of Newham as was I. The towering defender made his way to North London to ply his trade at Tottenham Hotspur who played at White Hart Lane. My parents settled in Tottenham, so close to the stadium - a twenty minute walk - that on a still and quiet Wednesday night when the wind was just right you heard the roar of their fans exhorting 'Come you Spurs!' or the weirdly unreconciled 'Yiddoes' with suedehead jack-booted exhilaration. Any wonder we called it 'White Hot Lane'. Any wonder we called it 'White Hot Lane'.  The parochialism, tribalism and even xenophobia bordering and frequently transgressing into racism in the terraces sharply juxtaposed with the growing cosmopolitanism on the field; something we were learning to come to terms with at a fever pitch. The team's coach passed along the roads I walked - [Ossie Ardiles](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osvaldo_Ardiles) and I exchanged a smile and wave as I made my way to school one morning - one of two exciting Argentines the club brought over in those days. Boys at school remarked my resemblance to him - I could be his younger brother they'd say. He and his colleague Ricky Villa would soon have to come to terms soon with *minding the gap* between their native and adopted homelands as they went to war. But they at least, like Sol and I will always be North London Forever. The team was famous for played with increasingly continental style and flair but alas, little end effect at least in terms of silverware (trophies). Those of us who banter call them 'Spurts' because of their inconsistency - ejaculations of pleasure, joy and the ecstasy of winning were rare among their true faithful.

The Emirates Stadium, Arsenal FC

By the time Sol joined and captained Spurs where he became their talismanic icon, North London had changed and we'd left my own private Jerusalem's pleasant pastures for Canada. They adored him - he was the bedrock of their defence - the foundation from which they sprung. Now, players are staked in the enterprise of their clubs; the ones with vision have opinions on how clubs should be run and progress - and many transition to express that insight long after they've stopped playing as managers and directors - some with [extraordinary effect](https://www.arsenal.com/news/edu-becomes-sporting-director) that dwarf even relatively successful 'journeyman' playing careers. They care about their club but obviously about their own careers; they want success which frankly, has little to do with silverware but rather the opportunity to express their art and science among *their* peers - those in their class and calibre; and it's a fact of life that we all bring different attributes and levels of commitment to whatever we do with varying outcomes; often only pure luck determines outcomes. There is too the desire to return to 'another beloved tribe' - I was at The Emirates (pictured above) sitting in his cubicle, the day Arsenal boy through-and-through (and [battle of the buffet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Buffet) pizza-throwing antagonist) Cesc Fabregas left for Barcelona. We bid him a sad farewell - it was his return to then successful Chelsea years later that grated a little. We would have laughed had he gone to Spurts.

**Faraway, So Close**

Sol wasn't a journeyman. Think 'main character vibe' - at the height of his career, he must have thought (and subsequent results validate his thinking) that it was time for him to move on. When he chose *Arsenal* - trauma and apprehension rippled through North London. 

Arsène Wenger** instigated the move. A journeyman at Strasbourg, Wenger found his true gift in coaching - at Monaco, then Grampas 8 and ultimately the club he'll always be most closely associated with. It was Spurs legend and Forever North Londoner, Glenn Hoddle who played under Wenger in Monaco that had the crucial hand in bringing him to 'home' by introducing him to then chairman David Dein. Wenger brough home the League title - our first in almost a decade and transformed the English game and the thought of Sol at the heart of Arsenal's defence vaulting them to ever more success angered them. The Spurs faithful appreciate a great player's dedication, commitment and service; they would have happily sent him off with fond wishes to anywhere - except Arsenal - a thirty minute drive separate the two. 

As, I came of age, in my suburban borough real or imagined distinctions drew me to one and not the other. In spite of my proximity to Spurs, I chose Arsenal who won my affection so early, that I can't say truly say when.

Spur for me were Faraway, so close. I chose Arsenal for the 'openness' it seemed to emanate; as I approached my teens, I often felt left on the 'outside'. My borough, Edmonton, was a different place in the early 1970s - white and working class and those were days of deep economic uncertainty and inflation. Meanwhile my Gujarati heritage ran deep at home even as I tried to blend in beyond it. I won't speak of other sub-cultures but mine to me at least emphasizes an a frugality, hard work, a voracious appetite for knowledge (what else makes life worth living?); there is too, a penchant for deliberately side-stepping of conflict - something I've come to understand has limits (and why I immersed myself in [Krav Maga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krav_Maga)). In any case, it leads to relative prosperity at least - my parents came to England with scarcely a hundred pounds and they created something approaching upwardly mobile middle-class in a single decade - the 1970s no less! I suppose it's hard for the indigenous 'other' to relate to an outsider's sudden success - the irony is that 'we' left Africa for the same reasons - the increasing sense of being a beleaguered prosperous tribe amidst the deprived. And yet, though I felt indigenous to London - that remarkably cosmopolitan citadel - and feeling an immense pride on school trips into its gloriously elegant core; I generally failed to be absorbed into the course and parochial suburbia immediately outside our shop; I process the trauma of rejection even today decades late; perhaps, the pain pours out now because I've reached 'half-time' in my life and the pause gives me time to reflect and process so that I may forge ahead in my 'second half' in the hope and faith that I belong everywhere instead of nowhere: *That I feel that I'm always home. That I'm never alone*. And yet looking back, the grandness of the city's core was built on exploitation and inequity of both it's own periphery - the suburbs and beyond it the North, the *other* home nations, and the world beyond it? How should I feel about that? How do I reconcile disappointment and pride when I wander, ride or drive through Londinium and with all *its* contradictions?

*London never sleeps, it just sucks The life out of me, and the money from my pocket London always creeps, showbiz hugs The life out of me, have some dignity honey* 

~ Catatonia ([Londinium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wp0Pqcj1Nis))

My own 'personality disorder' has been to be inside and outside simultaneously of so many tribes and that too having a paradox of a name that is at once 'everything' and 'nothing' So here I am, with the choice and realizing that the only viable narrative is to believe I'm everything, everywhere to everyone. It's why I obsess about these matters and why I want to help make it all work' why I want to make the red lights turn green endlessly for *everyone*.

**The Brady Bunch - A Blended Family**

A thirty-minute bus ride away Arsenal was still close *enough* from the home above our my dad's cornershop at 1 Kendal Parade. It was  a traditional English Newsagent and Confectionist - the sort you [drop in for a pack of cigarettes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGAOstUIhpU) at a busy node called the Cambridge Roundabout. I felt Arsenal felt close emotionally. It had pioneered the introduction of black and Irish players; my 'favourite' at the time was Republic of Ireland international - the genius of a midfielder Liam Brady (that too in the midst of [The Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles)); that's an official replica shirt of his - as OCD as ever, and a bit miffed with the Club, I removed 'Brady' the name. As well, the club feels and still feels thoughtful and cerebral - among its fans are creative artists and idealists - [Nick Hornby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Hornby), [Matt Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Lucas), [Gilles Peterson](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b01fm4ss), [Joe Strummer](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Strummer) and [Jeremy Corbyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Corbyn) - good company. Unlike Spurs we were anxiously introspective while we watched - our foes called our stadium at Highbury 'the Library'. 

The film [adaptation ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gGv8S1nqzdk)of Nick Hornby's introspective book *Fever Pitch* explores the gap between the 'manosphere' of football and its tenuous relationship with modern assertive femininity. 'You're being colonized' and 'It's all a form of sinister male manipulation' are two memorable lines from a friend as a Sarah (Ruth Gemmell) falls in love with Arsenal-obsessed Paul Ashworth (Colin Firth). The film's set in 1989 - a historic year for Arsenal, for football and for the world; one of despair and hope; of walls that [stood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_disaster) and [fell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall); neither of which were needed if we hadn't been institutionalized, infantilized and set upon each other.

Much has changed in English football to manage that manosphere and make the game accessible to the wider audiences it deserves not least is the development of the Women's game. As well on the 'terraces' where the fans live - thankfully it's generally for the better - they often mingle together happily. The gooner faithful too have found their voice in our 'new' [home](https://www.arsenal.com/the-club/emirates-stadium), The Emirates. However, decades ago when I 'mingled' at my first live match it was a frightening experience. It was the NLD (North London Derby) and I was around 10 or so. My dad asked a couple of local lads - they were Spurs, older and in their twenties, to chaperone me to the game. I was told to keep my paraphernalia at home, sit still and stifle enthusiasm for my team. The game was at Highbury and we were in the section reserved for visiting fans. The atmosphere throughout was heavily torpid - full of invective venom and animosity. Sensory overload kept me from focusing on the game - it was low scoring - I think a draw and the only real memory I have is of a young Arsenal fan, my age, tears streaming down his face being led away from an equally traumatic experience. He and his consoling father ran the gauntlet between the goal and raucous Spurs mob screaming course abuse. I came home stunned and to this day I can't remember whether with was the 1980 or 1981 home fixture.

**Solomon Arrayed In All His Glory**

It was to be a long twenty-five years before I returned to the Highbury in 2004 to watch another pivotal game - our last victory (against Aston Villa) in an unprecedented string of 49 League games played undefeated. Since  Arsène Wenger's instantly impactful revolution and renaissance began in 1996, Sol and his colleagues had been *Arrayed in Glory -* collecting silver and critical acclaim with attractive, flowing football rich in goals plundered and miserly in those conceded. Then, gathered with friends and family I watched us fall to defeat weeks later when we played Manchester United at Old Trafford. I watched the game at West Ham United's Upton Park - where my brother was getting married in East London where our lives had begun. I was moody that day after the loss - something I regret of course. I wasn't too different really from those Spurs fans years ago and I hope I didn't take too much away from the occasion we were there to celebrate. The game was always going to be hotly contested - one between the two titans in those years; The outcome was and still is hotly controversial; the pivotal moment was a 'foul' by Sol Campbell on Wayne Rooney (who dived). United won that day and we went on to suffer an immediate crisis from which we would recover - but in hindsight it was the beginning of a long 'down cycle' for my club - one from which we are only now emerging at an exhilarating pace. We're now playing with a surfeit of Gabriels and, occasionally, even Jesus. We play with fluidity and toil not *-* we are redeemed.

As I look back on the my own recent past, I had a moment of crisis in 2018 , and it's astonishing how quickly confidence founded on years of productive endeavour evaporate in a moment of volatility and how arduous the journey to rediscover oneself can be. But it's that process of suffering that are the making of us; or perhaps it's the revelation of deeper trauma that those wounds open - ones that we may then examine closely with introversion to truly come full circle, discover our Call to Adventure, and kick on with a swagger that only renewed integral strength give.

**Mind The Gap**

*Lying wide awake at night Sleeping in the morning light Doing all these things although I shouldn't be ashamed of them* 

~ The Mighty Lemon Drops ([Inside Out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bJr56j2yC7s))

My point throughout this piece is that Sol and many of us who grew up being international bright young things' as the walls seemingly began to come down around the world are threading the connective tissue between ourselves and an ever more complex planet as we become a single wandering tribe upon it. He and I belong to *both* North London clubs in the end - they and the both the joy and pain they brought made us. So to are we both of England, despite our colour, and thence World and to our God. There's no conflict there for us - no need to pick a 'tribe'. The challenge is profound for those of us whose deep pluralism is founded on bedrock of values (creative yield) and a life experience that is the proof of those values. We are 'joins' - the evermore distributed secure nodes - in the corpus of humanity carrying the richly rewarding though taxing responsibility of explaining the 'other' to each so that we may come function and thrive in creative and procreative productivity for all eternity. In the interstitial, transitional states between tribes, we feel discomfort, embarrassment, perhaps even shame; and with that comes the trauma of ostracism. We may dwell in anxiety and depression but never guilt - we have harmed no one - even as the world sanctions us for not 'choosing a side' or 'being tourists'. Forever, we will turn our hearts inside out as we feel the strain of *beings* having a *human* 'in the body' experience - as we *mind the gap* between ourselves and the humanity and seek to lead by serving. My refrain forever will be those of that other Englishman and [renegade of funk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4KXdU3cZbNQ) Tom Paine - 'The World is my Country, to do good my Religion'. I began thinking about these ideas early and throughout my life subsequently as I was constantly immersed in one new tribe after another. I recall the first time I really paid attention in class - when I *finally* lifted my bored skull; when Ms. Bays wrote on a primary school blackboard: 'Communism' and 'Capitalism' denoting the secular faiths of two warring tribes - our modern-day Sparta and Athens; the grand binary of our macro personality disorder and as-yet unreconciled collective but pluralistic souls. It is the chronic stress that wears out and fragments our planet.

**The Chemicals Between Us**

Tottenham Hotspur more so than Arsenal claim a Jewish heritage that even in my young mind I couldn't reconcile with the racist skinheads who supported the club in my youth - at least in my proximity. I was born and raised in Jewish neighbourhoods and am of a clan with deep natural and organic alignment. I wonder at the combination of the nature and nurture of my genetic, cultural and even atomic* make-up. Those strange chants of 'Yiddo' - both racist and yet proudly adoptive - from the Spurs terraces represent the highly concentrated distillation of inner personal conflict for me. At Oakthorpe my closest friends were the quite thoughtful Jewish kids as well as the sons of immigrant Turkish and Greek ones and the exotically named Franco-Polynesian Cheyenne (as a fellow gooner, Star Wars and Indiana Jones enthusiast he naturally became my best mate). We gave the rough 'white' boys a wide berth; my schoolmates were too young for racism - we'd all grown up together. But soon we'd be split into new collectives of varying academic pedigree as High School approached. 

**All That Rugby Puts Hairs on your Chest** 

Latymer the smart Grammar school was beyond me - I was still mostly a lazy daydreamer at school - and I was bound for the hard 'middling' [Winchmore](https://www.winchmore.enfield.sch.uk/) to entertain new rough 'white' boys who weren't the brothers our surrogate fathers Messrs. Cumberland-Price and Mainstone had had a hand in raising. Yet another gap to mind. We rumbled with [Minchenden ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minchenden_Grammar_School)on the commons; press-ganged, I joined and threw the odd fist - a way to group affiliate - and release pent-up suburban ennui; pre-adolescent gangs of North London. The laughs, broad grins and accolades having dropped a fella or two and coming away from these mêlées were dopamine fixes - the 'distinctions' fell away and you were bonded. Goal celebrations at an NLD. Nobody died - no knives or sticks - it was (almost) harmless 'rugby' without refs. This is the purpose of sport - to satiate our post-thymotic, 'end of history' impulses ([Fukuyama](https://francis fukuyama end of history) wasn't wrong - just early). We should play more and watch less in multiple arenas developing cross-functional capability preferably with clear 'red lines' delineating humanity's universal values. Much like my Oakthorpe and Winchmore report cards, if I were to complete one for humanity it would read 'Must do better'.

**You're The One For Me** **Fati**

I have often expressed in my life a wildness occasionally breaching 'reasonable' boundaries of 'self-interest' - a dyslexic self-annihilating chapter I call 'Hairway to Steven' coming close to throwing the proverbial 'baby out with the bathhouse water'. It was a heady dive into Hades - Never mind [Zero to One](https://transition-insight.com/f/money---e-pluribus-unum-0) - it was less than zero - my private instance of the chaotic 'end of times' now absorbing the world. All of this has been to 'offset' my natural introversion and to explore *legitimate* boundaries - even those well beyond my natural inclinations - how far do these boundaries go that do not involve harming 'the other'? As for what was in it for me - it was yet more exhilarating post-thymos - way more than *anyone* needs. I'm dialing well back now and keen to calibrate for what I *objectively need:* An expression of clear 'self-interest' as *the* defining priority in life - I'm minding the gap between me and the places, things and ones that *God* wants in my life. I need only my Lord God whose love I have in abundance and whose gift imparted upon me (and to you for that matter) is to transmit it to *the whole of the world*; that ordinary world is bounty enough for me. I aim with stoicism to accept with grace whatever it returns to me; *Amor Fati*

*'Chemicals between Us' by Bush (who came from Shepherd's Bush - down the road from where I grew up) suggests we're entangled to our surroundings. Each of us is a microcosm of (cerebral) computing power in relative states of stillness (flow in whatever creative actions absorbs us) interpreting the data (energy and matter) that is around us (again) in flow, so that we may illuminate truth and beauty (satya and sundar), living with such monist vision as to extinguish dualities - the distinctions between the one and the other.

****Wenger is a Germanic name. He hails originally from Alsace-Lorraine - a region France and Germany once traded back and forth at the cost of great blood and treasure. A scrap of earth, like The Crimea for which innocents and combatants are needlessly being sent to their deaths and that too by Slavic brothers was once a place of relative co-habitation. So it was with Alsace-Lorraine. Europeans happily live and travel through it without much thought today. Just two more gaps that require careful minding by competent and pragmatic visionaries.
