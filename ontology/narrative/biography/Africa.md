## London Life



>North London forever
>Whatever the weather
>These streets are our own
>And my heart will leave you never
>My blood will forever
>Run through the stone
>
>~ Louis Dunford (North London Forever)



My parents, newly married in Kenya, arrived in London to start life anew in 1967 from Africa. 

My dad was born in the tidy little town of Eldoret, high up in the Rift Valley; the cradle of human civilization. He was the youngest of five brothers and two sisters. Riding his bicycle to school, he might happen upon a giraffe or a wildebeest in much the same way you or I might come across a raccoon or squirrel.

Now 83 he reminisces about darting across the road in town whenever he spied an “auntie” approaching, hastily intent, no doubt, on imparting an impromptu yet thoroughly gruelling and tortuous examination of his times tables or spelling. Indignant that she wasn’t authorised by the Empire to carry out such interrogations, he would make his escape before being spied within earshot. His father was a banker working for Standard Charter in their local branch. Tall and fair, always besuited with pipe and pocket watch he had all the bearings of a patrician and would have easily made the grade as London looked for colonial talent to match Britannia's mercantile opportunism. Even in the dying days of Empire there was a sprawl of red to manage and steadily unwind. He was someone they recognized and counted as one of their own. 

My mother was born in Kisumu, the picturesque town on Lake Victoria that Barrack Obama's father hailed from. 



It was the source of the Nile, from where a fertile imagination might envisage humanity setting out up the river and onward, out of Africa, thousands of years ago.

My grandfather was himself an adventurous man who left India in the years between the century’s great wars. He made clear to his own father his intention to emigrate to Brazil. His father, piqued at the audacity, tore up the travel documents. When his fury settled, the two of them agreed he might instead depart for Africa, on the steamboats that plied the ancient route between Bombay and Mombasa.
