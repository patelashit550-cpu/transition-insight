### Left Write

Central to the nature of governance and decision-making in general is ***bias***, which is the subject of this piece. Specifically: What counts as an innocent enough ***preference*** and at what point does that preference compound to become an arbitrary prejudice: Something we call ***dogma*** or ***ideology*** - respectively these are theological or secular ideas deemed to be self-evidently valid and which require neither explanation nor investigation.

**Preferences** are grounded on a liberal principle - autonomy; the idea that people *should* be free to self-actualize to do as they wish; in other words apply their innate sense of curiosity to pursue their idea of happiness and accomplish what they feel they need to during their lifetime.

To be broadly credible, our preferences must seem reasonable from another's perspective. The most credible preferences are (today, at least) our innate tendencies, such as laterality—or side preference. This dictates which hand we default to for tasks like writing. While roughly 10% of the population write with their left hand, a subtle, benign coercion socially engineers many left-handers to develop cross-dominance (the neurological assignment of different tasks to different hands, rather than a single dominant side).

The question of whether this cross-dominance is intrinsic or an accommodation to environmental conditions may not be particularly important. However, it raises a deeper question as to how many such adjustments we all make simply to fit in. This speaks directly to our susceptibility as social animals to accepting arbitrary prejudice or entrenched systemic frameworks. Much like **Metcalfe’s Law** in network dynamics, the dominance of a social norm scales with the square of the number of people who adopt it—generating overwhelming **network effects** that compel individual compliance, even with systems we would prefer were otherwise. From my own (anarchist) lens the idea of national identity is both arbitrary and contentious - particularly in the culturally heterogeneous Five Eyes countries: United States, Great Britain, Canada, Australia and New Zealand.

### Natural Order

Just as left-handed people organically and without any real sense of grievance fit into a social world created largely by the right-handed folk we all by and large align ourselves with biologically driven biases to effect what amounts to a form of *organic* or anarchic governance. (I make a distinction between governance and government - an anarchic government is an oxymoron but any demonstrably beneficial personal regime is at least through my own lens anarchic *governance* - namely the application of autonomy and agency in the pursuit of personal future value in the present. It is in a sense generative of value.)

An example: Most of us need seven to nine hours of sleep but as a species, we are hardwired to rest when the sun goes down. When we are well-adjusted and unburdened by artificial light, our natural instinct is simply to move with the day and wind down as darkness falls - about 90% of us sleep through the night rather than through the day. Most of that other 10% make the adjustment due to some personal circumstance (the job requires it for example or they suffer from a sleep disorder).

The darkness of night is fraught with danger for the simple reason that you can't see as well as during the day and it is colder. What you have here is a bias grounded in ecology - it works towards our personal self-interest and those of our community by reducing the footprint requiring governance and as well the expenditure of energy in all forms. The *yoga element* of our ecology is in a sense inhaling or drawing in to conserve, restore and replenish.  What we have here is decision making in the *architecture* or a design for life where utility for both one and the group is aligned to each other *and* the ecology.

In another time or place we imposed curfews limiting people from leaving their homes at night because it reduces the cost of governance where the capital simply isn't available to secure people the luxury of night time security beyond their personal perimeter.

Our desire for efficiency therefore makes sustainability and conservation our primary objectives because they make life easier and more pleasant - the expenditure of time, energy and materials (known as the triple constraints in project management) comes down to money. The more efficient we are the less money we need to live the good life.

What does a *credible* personal preference, a bias look like in the real world - one that is *universally* seen as ideal? Generally they are optimizations which become personal protocols (self-governance). This derives from a common-sense assessment of practical situational considerations in our personal space and geography and a keen awareness of our *embodied* needs.

These then cause an ecology to generate resilience or as we say in information technology harden, into a standard that works to an acceptable standard. Something we call social norms - and we apply a certain amount of pressure on others to conform to them while we are together in *public* because it makes everyone's life less unpredictable or volatile within that particular perimeter.

### Technique Is Necessary

Our decisions, if not the things which compel them, are our own and notwithstanding our integrated global context and the very real and immediate impact we have in disparate places on countless people we've never met every decision we make is *local* - obviously because we make them *where* we are.

When we ourselves were personally responsible for meeting basic needs in primitive conditions we tended towards efficiency and minimalism. The situation often the dire and existential need of ensuring the survival of our group focused one's attention and concentration on one's work one task at a time with a clear sense of both purpose and priorities. This literally provided what we call in SDLC a backlog. Everything is not a priority - just the first item on the backlog - sometimes also called a *Kanban* board.

A natural *order* of actionable tasks with clear outcomes whose weight was determined through discussion is defined. This results in flow because even when conditions are not ideal assuming one is of course interested in living, well-marshaled and mentored, we are attracted and interested to the task; everyone is *sympatico*.

The principle at play here is *alignment* to community and ecology. Specifically this is a ***normative* cognitive bias**. We are not neutral nor indifferent - we have preferences.

In modernity however a **technological/environmental bias**; a combination of tooling and societal comforts alters our behavior; we cede autonomy and agency.

There are been many instances of people who complacent in their delegation of situational awareness and distractedly ruminating on some repetitive idea anchored in the past or the future gets turned in the wilderness; suddenly alone and unprepared they begin to panic and then despair; confidence and morale declines rapidly as they fall victim to something as innocuous and ordinary - hypothermic weather (10°C–15°C). This in some senses, as cruel as it may sound, this is *karmic* - one reaps what one sows. Present value needs to be anchored in the *current* moment if it is to grow from experience and generate some hypothetical future value - whatever it was one gleaned from being attentive in creation.

### [working] Heuristics

Biases may be seen as a heuristic. A heuristic is a short cut which simplifies a protocol based on evidence provided by one's own experience. Recall that a protocol is an algorithm or process used to make optimal decisions. Heuristics are frequently referred to as 'rule of thumb'. It's an approximation or generalization.

rather than a precise rule—which provides efficiencies by applying organic inclinations contextually; these may be innately personal or developed by a community. This we call *bias* and it is often synonymous with common sense and consistency. We start with our own idiosyncratic or eccentric behaviour different from others.

The question is whether we make it a habit to routinely validate our biases to examine whether they still result in optimal decision.

We have a barometer in climate which tells us that something isn't quite right about the way we operate in the ecology. It would be one thing if we were generally contented but even the most wealthy are often not particularly happy. In the natural world I apprehend general contentment in the anarchy of the natural world - a pleasant synergy that is absolutely wonderous to behold. Even when it is fierce it is beautiful.

### [working] The social network and energy

Why is objectivity important on a social network? Because efficiency and the conservation of energy and matter is the base factor for assessing credibility (which is to machines what in humanist terms is deemed legitimacy).

This then raises the question of how we use energy on the network and whether its application benefits us to another's detriment or disadvantage. Is it for example used to deliberately impede others without being particularly about which others. Are we gaming the network so that our content is served up ahead of others or are we willing to commit to the natural order?

In short the manner in which we extract and consume energy and matter is *the* key denominator in determining the degree to which a node (whether autonomous or human) may be trusted by its counterparties including obviously intermediaries or bystanders. Nobody would want to be party to a transaction even as an IP (internet protocol) hop which isn't at least neutral (does no harm) from a holistic perspective. Increasingly however nodes are being created to be safe and secure channels through the presentation of software stack which operates as part of a transactionally secure channel that is *globally* available and therefore *universally* appreciated. That is the aim for this domain (website).
