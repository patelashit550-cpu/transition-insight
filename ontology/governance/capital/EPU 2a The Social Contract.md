## Introduction

Part Two of *E Pluribus Unum* examines the social contract as the blueprint for our collective behaviour. While Part One, *Leviathan*, mapped how hierarchical power is maintained in democracies, we now scrutinise the myth sustaining it: that meritocratic hierarchies naturally emerge to serve the common interest. This myth envisions institutions automatically elevating the capable through a transparent marketplace of ideas, moderated by unbiased, well-informed citizens. By "myth," I do not mean a lie, but a foundational ideal projected by the sovereign (unum) to the many (pluribus).

In reality, the complexity of the modern state makes this ideal unreachable. Consequently, we default to two practical contracts:  the law and the acceptance of legal tender (money) in exchange for labour. These are the primary technologies through which the commonwealth or domain maintains order and its related aesthetic element - continuity.

From this starting point, self-interest for most people manifests in two ways: obedience to the law—and the social norms underpinning it—and the accumulation of wealth to secure well-being in the present and into an undefined future. By navigating these standard social contracts, the individual secures the social standing and the wherewithal required to enjoy life with contentment. Essentially the self and the sovereign are aligned on interests - at least within context of their common jurisdiction - the *geographic* space they share.

This arrangement begins to fail however when aspects of personal dignity, privacy for example, are distributed as privilege rather than as ordinary reciprocal courtesies—granted to some and withheld from others based on their position within evolving hierarchies and a sense of whose stature and position merits defending in what is essentially a pecking order. To some degree, this is both natural and even *rational* behaviour. For example, in terms of our public demeanour we expect of one another a *basic* standard of hygiene, attire, and decorum as a condition of civil participation; we likewise demand charisma, expertise and erudition from those we defer to as leaders. Conversely, on the other hand we tend—rightly or wrongly—to stigmatise the coarse, unhygienic, besotted, or haggard; those who struggle to remain accountable to themselves, let alone to wider society. We deem them a danger to personal safety and hygiene; both ours as well as theirs and in ideal circumstances we steer them towards an adherence to our collective standard while giving them a wide berth as and when we encounter them.

Yet even as these expectations are set, a pervasive competition for status—or ‘flex’—emerges among those privileged enough to enjoy what would be considered by any measure relative comfort and wealth. This competition is natural, even primal; we preen, swagger, signal, and gloat to attract one another and consolidate position. As well a psychic distance develops between those who 'have' and those who 'have not' - one so substantial that empathy is lost as various notions of 'the other' develop both within and certainly without the domain.

For the liberal, pluralist and thoughtful - those who through curiosity or life circumstance have traversed domains, self-awareness and conscience forces a choice: either they compete within hierarchy for position, title, and capital or decamp when reason dictates and the work no longer provides an opportunity for personal growth or has any meaning. At some point the reckoning arrives because the work is no longer appealing; or that other social contract legal tender no longer sufficiently merits the trade-offs implied in what David Graeber once called a bullshit job. We are after all not just employees but ourselves consumers and customers and when we don't see any value in what we are creating we have to stop because the social contract has failed.

Contracts are created as a utility—an instrument—to allow complex civilisations to flourish. The word is derived from Latin: the prefix *con-* from *cum* (together), and *tract* from *trahere*, to draw or pull. A contract is thus a means by which we draw together as a single party to gain traction and manifest a result for mutual benefit—something greater than we could achieve separately.

In complex society, where the vast majority of those in close proximity are strangers, two technologies must function well to secure peace, order, and good government: law and money. One demarcates our lanes; the other greases the tracks. Without good governance—laws fairly and equitably applied—these instruments falter.

Contracts are *creative*. They facilitate generation and productivity—a kind of fertility: something apparently from nothing, beginning with the seed of an idea expressed between persons. The sovereign may be seen as a *semper idem*, a semantic perimeter embodied; yet it is the virtual identity of Leviathan that delineates a uniform notion of law and the broadly adopted social norms that accompany it. In the body politic, laws function as autonomous reflexes, while social norms are habits formed over millennia—protocols constituting the corpus of our collective tradition.

Together, law and social norm form the substrate upon which value is recognised and exchange becomes possible; they constitute the conditions of legitimacy under which goods and services acquire standing.

To create such goods and services, each of us must understand meaning in a *personal* context. Introversion turns us toward a primal quality from which rational utility may be derived, allowing our unique talents to manifest in the real world as something aesthetically and practically valued. With that insight, we may congregate to form a society that preserves belonging while sustaining our commonwealth and ecology. There is a tension here between our contentment which is tied to the preservation of our global commons (clean air, water and soil for example) and our commitment to belonging to a domain; Domains optimise for internal success, often by externalising environmental costs. Because the global commons are shared and diffuse, no single domain bears full responsibility for their degradation. Thus, loyalty to one’s domain can conflict with the collective restraint necessary to preserve the commons upon which long-term contentment depends.

Now, within the complex society that constitutes a domain, individuals gather in nodes, or communities, and their contributions are finely specialised—atomistic components that are brought together as goods and services which are then transacted in community. That value is actualised insofar as it is recognised as legitimate within the commonwealth when its supply is readily met with a demand. *Ideally* the individual living in a community or domain but obviously as well in the context of a global commons seeks to maximise personal interests while aligning them with the domain's *and* the global commons. Any misalignment of interests will be dissonant and functionally disturbing; it signals a breakdown in the social contract's core utility. When this dissonance is sustained, the friction shifts from a cognitive load to a somatic breach. The persistent requirement to *simulate* agency within an extractive, non-functional loop induces chronic stress responses that manifest as systemic physiological decay. In this state, the social contract is no longer merely inefficient—it is pathogenic, demanding a biological toll that ultimately compromises the individual's vitality, sense of worth and capacity to live at all.

Having a Calling allows one to transcend the domain's demands when they cannot be reconciled with creation and a true understanding of what it means to be alive and content beyond possessing the basic vitality afforded by good physiological health. It amounts to having the agency to affect *legitimate* animus. What do we mean by legitimate animus? It is to express to others clearly what constitutes for oneself the boundary of legitimate behaviour from *them* towards us. For example a mother's snarling teeth-bared hostility towards a potential predator who trespasses too closely while she minds her cubs. In a sense she is expressing her own instinctive primal calling. In humanity, far more complex and variant, the expression of one's Calling amounts to manifesting one's true nature in a myriad number of ways which include unique talents and capabilities that derive from a profoundly unique combination of attributes and characteristics. It is an insistence on self-actualisation, which might be *perceived* as animus (offensive) even though it amounts to a liveness or *anima*: an aspect of individual form and personality developed over time but now expressed outwardly in ways that seemingly contradict others. As we contradict some and yet affirm others, so we find community and thus the natural space for the development of social contract in a pluralist context.

Calling is more than a vocation; it is the whole set of qualities that combines to give one their *unique* personality. It requires a seeker - someone deeply curious about their environment to realise it and understands life as a journey of self-discovery in the context of their particular circumstances alone and without comparison, contrast or reference to others.

As well it requires a fine balance between a contentment with one's current state of affairs and a yearning for what could be; this may be viewed metaphorically as a path between the present and the future navigated steadily and without excessive toil.

This quality which increasingly over time is imbued with *ease* but which invariably, at least at the outset is characterised by ambition which while providing torque and progress is offset by the friction of ego. Ego is acquisitive and its penchant for accumulation of things contradicts the seeker's deeper intention and progress because it impairs the development of autonomy and agency. Calling sounds philosophically esoteric or too spiritual and as such unsuitable as framework for serious analysis. So let's examine its relevance in architectural terms for capital (value) and social contracts.

Calling requires presence and what has often been called *flow* - a deep connection to a creative endeavour in the present moment - now without thoughts of what result might in the future be won. What gives it this quality is that as an endeavour it is geared or tethered to creation or as it is often called in secular terms the global commons. This tethering through Calling of an individual to those global commons is a beacon which signals to the world right purpose and intention; it is a *tell* and whatever work is being milled by this node is *evidently* of value. It can be said to have proof of work. One may not directly see the series of transactions or interactions that ultimately emanate from the one who has discovered calling but the outcome or proof is nonetheless evident. This is known as a signal or the beacon effect. In analogue terms this beacon effect is a form of charisma; a reflection of what the self-sovereign self has in its sights which is the *global* commons or creation. As such its attractiveness is universally appealing; its single-minded focus on creation (ecology) evidence of deep *structural* integrity derived from a fundamental alignment to the whole. The phrase *E Pluribus Unum* itself reflects the idea of individuals being directed through nodality towards the global commons to feel a connected and a collective sense of being even as they emanate self with unimpeded autonomy to create something meaningful as its own reward first and foremost. Here you have the idea that one has first an obligation to meet before a privilege may be returned as due consideration for it.                   

This phenomenon—often termed the Beacon Effect—occurs when an agent first produces some visible “proof of work” that is illegible or opaque to casual participants but highly salient to a specific group, such as custodians, gatekeepers, or interested observers of systemic governance; these however  see it instead as a "proof of stake" or because the work is expressive of normative preferences or values; namely it is staked in the idea of alignment between the self and the global commons and aims to engender this idea through domains (the node). This lack of neutrality implies that it prefers something with yield over something without it. All else equal a higher yielding transaction has always had greater utility in a domain. 

This is anything that is in relative terms produced in a highly productive manner - which is why something high-quality or in architectural terms high-signal and pointed towards sustaining the global commons yet produced without any expectation of due consideration is highly prized by the individuals, autonomous agents (for example AI) and by extension the network itself as it utilises it. In the digital commons emitting a frequency of thought that is costly to produce, difficult to imitate and yet profoundly creative and beneficial across the spectrum of individual, sovereign and ecological interests makes a beacon visible to anyone with a sufficiently comprehensive view of time series data with which to plot its journey. 

Take for example what we're trying to do here in this work. The modern digital landscape is saturated with low-entropy noise — not only spam and ads, but also the endless churn of outdated corporate documentation constantly accumulated and curated in domains without evident purpose and to no end: material that nobody has any particular use for but is afraid to permanently remove. In that context the emergence of a *high-fidelity signal*, say good original and credible content fills an ontological need as an essential coordinate for the resonant social and intellectual navigation of all data (and as such value) for our entire network. Calling operates this way. It doesn't just exist but does so with intention, purpose and meaning. This is because, in a sense, and we are speaking in terms of metaphors here, *Calling* signals or beacons through and beyond community towards the global commons or creation - our broader ecological reality - which we all care about as the literal foundation for life. When we express Calling therefore we feel aligned and have direction as though while sailing or paddling and constantly triggered by the distracting low-entropy noise in a busy harbour we have set our own sights on a beacon or some other visual *mark* against which to tack and move towards in a measured undistracted manner, signaling 

This signaling acts as a structural handshake; it transforms the actor from an anonymous participant into a recognisable node - a pseudonymous  abstraction or address which interacts on the network by getting or posting information. The rock-steady character of what is built over time through repeated behaviour is what I call the Stone Rose in our ontology. The metaphor fits because  anthropological and archaeological strata of stone rose to reflect layered human activity, preserving the imprint of domains and sovereigns long since folded into history.

In the reality of our *digital* global commons, the layering of individual interactions which create identity is recorded as the braided sequence of transactions in a proof of work ledger —  Bitcoin. Proof-of-stake protocols Ethereum and Solana, also emerged in its aftermath. They extend the model by evaluating not just the expenditure of effort for a transaction, but its relative worth or opportunity cost across the spectrum of individual, community and sovereign interests and, *implicitly* also the global commons.

These protocols protect vested participants by increasing the difficulty and cost of predatory behaviour relative to its potential reward. It may be seen as a quiet expression of legitimate—or *defensive*—animus which has evolved over millennia to be deeply encoded. Similarly the application of unique talents to harvest a common good without extracting a toll from the commons itself. The hard shell of the long-lived turtle, and its mastery of ocean currents that allow it to travel vast distances with efficiency rather than speed, offer a useful example. The turtle does not *intend* to starve the adversaries who come knocking, nor does it posture or showboat to goad onlookers into understanding the futility of pursuit as it moves with ease beneath the ocean’s surface. It simply *prevails*—excelling with cool, competent indifference. What the turtle or any other animal widely admired by humans does *instinctively* is express a character honed over millennia through its connection to the ecology and consequent adaptation to it so that it comes to excel with seemingly effortless ease in the midst of creation - in ways that we find marvellous. It's as though the creature manifests a form of unperceived *aikido* that allows it to dwell in contentment within a world that yet predates upon it.

In civil society, one expression of defensive animus expressed by us collectively is the monopoly on violence we delegate to our law enforcement agencies. So too the smart attire and firm politeness that hints at competent coercion. It works - but only insofar as that power is exercised with measured restraint and care. That too consistently and almost unfailingly. Only then does it grant the domain legitimacy and earn the trust of those they are meant to protect and serve. That too only within a relatively organic and evolved regime which enjoys generally widespread credibility *beyond* its jurisdiction. Public confidence is hard earned and all too easily lost.

Humanity in its initially primitive conditions had as its own particularly fearsome qualities a particular kind of vision that combined intelligence and imagination to unique physical characteristics such as opposable thumbs. This ability allows us to mentally simulate paths towards alternative realities and then choose one that led to a safer more secure reality. It allowed us to master our physical circumstances in short order and even coax the fierce loyalty and friendship, and thus also the labour, of other creatures in that ambition.

In short order we used this ability to thrive in small communities within which considerable freedom though available had the protective aspects of a guarded paternalism wary of the primal instincts of the predatory beyond the collective's defined perimeter. Assets, intellectual and material were generally possessed communally and a shared sense of participation was imparted to members. These were the first social contracts: unstated and unspoken arrangements which operated as a network of bilateral relationships collectively reinforcing each other to form a web of protective social scaffolding to define closeness which, as it happens, more or less mirrored physical closeness.

In practical terms they were no less formal for being unwritten though of course their communalism negated the need for the exactitude in accounting for property that modern statutes provide. You can see then how closely tied law and money are because law, when it extends beyond the protection of the physical self amounts to a framework for protecting society's capital. This *order* or structure is elegantly generated in thought so seamlessly that we don't even notice it. These cues were the external outputs of a deeper internal process: that mental simulation that manifested outwardly as a measure of confidence and the capacity to work with what was available in the world in order to live life. All thought, at its root, amounts to such a simulation—a series of conditional statements which, given a set of constants and variables, predicts a result and then attempts to confirm it in reality through action but which first *signals* it with emotion (or if you will, instinct or intuition) - a complex preview with which we affirm to each other readiness to come together and form community - a *de facto* social contract with which to make light work of the matters that lie between us and the persistence of a nascent tribe.

The selection of a simulated scenario which is actuated and results in an incrementally new reality by two or more parties amounts to a rationalisation of opportunity cost and the emergence and completion of a *de facto* social contract. Once a successful path is executed we may repeat it until it is compiled into what we call *second* nature. This is nature—the real ecology—augmented now by a model of order, a superimposed imprint of technology or 'know-how' used to master our collective condition which has utility so that life may be more resonant or attractive (beautiful). 

This then amounts to the synthesis of a collective equivalent to our individual modelling of reality derived from the  discussion of our primal, deep, and rational elements and results in the generation of community and culture through an ongoing dialectic in civilisation akin to A/B testing in software development where a small change to the existing application (potential utility) is tested in the marketplace (truth) with a subset of users; an innovation derived through orthopraxy (practise) is tested against an existing orthodoxy to confirm relative utility. One common and rather trivial example of this process you frequently encounter is on your app store with the opportunity to accept and download a beta version of an application.

*Resonant* changes made to that beta version, that is to say those which are intuitively preferred are then absorbed into the alpha as a 'new normal' for release into the marketplace. In this broadest sense, this process mirrors for society and indeed civilisation an individual's journey towards Calling. 

Now, the question is whether our modern networks are geared towards directing each of us to our Calling so that our social contracts may then be broadly beneficial as each of us finds optimal flow in work chosen autonomously based on our own individual preferences. Currently it is arguable that the nodes governing our transactional behaviour whether online or in the real world have their own interests to protect even as they are treated as trusted parties - while we each all have our own. The prevailing zero-trust posture in information management certainly implies as much. In our current state we ourselves are responsible and accountable for self-preservation because even as we live in welfare states that purport to offer providence they themselves are mired in debt that stems ultimately from a failure in governance and the two foundational contracts: law and money. Meanwhile as they seek to find ways to maintain their own domain perimeters sustainably, the global commons go wanting.

This is an atomised condition not particularly conducive to collaboration and the use of contracts which are in any case ever more complex as they seek to account for every circumstance foreseeable or otherwise. It is high-entropy and may even be seen as a Hobbesian struggle; a war of everyone against everyone else; each one of us a *peer amid skim*. If our society or even civilisation is to work for all of us it stands to reason that it *encourage* autonomy in the midst of interdependence and so too must our contracts encourage participation rather than instinctively. That participation may be said to be framed within a mythology of jurisdiction; an idea which evolved dramatically in the twentieth century with the first migrations of post-colonial populations and then too through the advent of technologies which made the sharing of intellectual capital seamless and inexpensive.

The augmented reality of community and culture is an agreed upon modelling of the world. It is *de facto* social contract which over time evolves into *de jure* (or formal) social contracts to the extent that we succeed in expressing ideas which have such collective appeal that we feel obliged to articulate them formally into bounded volumes that mirror and celebrate reality:  art, literature, song and so forth. This amounts to the creation of mythology in its broadest anthropological sense. 

However the mythology of jurisdiction in particular is the modelling of community through which we create a story or an idea of who we are collectively as a people so that we may function as though we were a single organism without much thought or introspection as to our constitution. This is the dictionary of our commonality - a semantic perimeter we call Jurisdiction within which we may function with an evolved sense of security or *prudence* such that we are not constantly in fear for our safety or in any sense distracted in some deep thought about the nature of our reality. Indeed the development of formal social contracts in law is generally referred to as jurisprudence. Moreover the basis or starting point for that sense of prudence is not strictly contractual but, as suggested earlier emotional, instinctive and intuitive; it is a sense of *belonging* to one another and being responsible to one another which underlies all notions of community and social contract.

Civilisation then evolves such that we develop ever more complexity in symbol and language with which to create the story of who we are which then serves to bind us to one another ever closer. Furthermore this mythology of jurisdiction is imbued or grounded within particular climes, landscapes, flavours, fragrances and so forth; and most of all a collective sense of time and memory - of what we have done together and as well what has been done to us. This constitutes a primal and rational reality made coherent with a *model* developed through collective dialogue and interactive introspection and deep introversion. Mythology implies story-telling but the kind which envelops fundamental truths in light, colour and magic so that they may persist long in our consciousness and in those of others that encounter us. No matter how larger than life and vivid this mythology, no jurisdiction long survives the obviously fragile, fraudulent and deceitful. No mythology of jurisdiction long sustains and tolerates broad lies told on its behalf by those who claim to personify it. In some sense the mythology of jurisdiction is propaganda that is found to be additive. They are the truths we emphasise and tell each other most often.

In time humanity created countless communities legitimised by the very fact of their instantiation in that moment when the kernel of viscerally *encoded* specie called humanity was emanated into the real world as signal and then through the awareness and intention of those carrying it made to persist. Community morphed into society and culture and ultimately the complex and profoundly diverse nation-states of our present moment. One aspect of the mythology of jurisdiction was the subsequent encoding of formal and informal cues which signalled to each of us our place and standing within a *hierarchy* approximating merit where merit was measured in terms of the guile and capital at one's disposal. This capital was required to both maintain collective hierarchical order and one's own standing within it. 

That capital itself (money) is a collective rationalisation of how utility is derived using resources from our environment. In moments of utilitarianism versus reverence we see the natural world as a storage 'unit' (une commode) for commodities. To the extent we gauge a utility and the need to extract value from our ecology to that extent also we lose something worthy of reverence - the beauty of the natural world. This isn't a flight of fancy. We all see what a patch of wilderness looks like before and after some resource is quarried from it. Whatever application is made of that resource, something certainly is removed in the way of quality in our lives as we look out at a space with the recollection of what it once was. What we are in fact drawing from  as we extract material from our environment is the mythology of jurisdiction in material terms; that ephemeral quality in our space and time which makes existing within it worthwhile.

A hierarchical structure—often also described as a pyramid—is a profoundly stable and in many ways efficient model. It has served civilisation for millennia allowing governance to scale rapidly because of its capacity to clarify answers quickly for those in leadership. Decision-making is straightforward on key questions: who is accountable for setting the vision and direction for the domain, and to whom responsibility is *delegated* to execute that vision as the road-map evolves and projects, components, and tasks are identified. Historically, that vision was oriented toward *perpetuating* the existing order beyond one’s own lifespan. This was certainly the case in past feudal systems as well as the early parliamentary monarchies they evolved into. 

Historically, the most persistent systems—persistence being an imperfect but useful heuristic for resilience—are those that bind decision-makers to *outcome-based accountability* as judged by relevant domain stakeholders. England and France stand out as regimes in which the base has routinely executed regime changes from the base of the pyramid when personal *kompromat* metastasised into structural failure through incremental compromise. Russia similarly decapitated swiftly to staunch the haemorrhaging of governance which began with the Romanov dynasty being responsible for the West's first great humiliation since antiquity in 1905 with Russia's defeat to Japan. Persistence or continuity requires ideally internal structural cohesion (solidity) coupled with a surface opacity (zero-knowledge) which nevertheless allows for a satisfied yeoman class. By this we mean that those steeped in artisan and agricultural arts and science on the outside of the regime but inside the domain and the receiving end of governance diktat could still make arithmetical sense of value and price in their world which after all was their primary concern - to be far removed from the machinations of state to experience life and culture in their own mode.

Even modern so-called democratic government which hasn't fundamentally evolved since World War II may be framed as hierarchical. Certainly during war itself they operated in a total command-and-control framework; one which extended right down to the most fundamental form of eminent domain:  conscription and the pressing of men into war and the arbitrary expression of violence against others which is the very antithesis of self-actualisation. As well after the War if anything the scope of government's reach has extended to tax income and establish dependency for 'health', education and a degree of so-called 'social security'. Perhaps wartime 'solidarity' left a residual nostalgia for the ways in which we rallied to achieve collective aims such that we retained a taste for the  comforting relative certitude that clear lines of command provide and were amenable therefore to sustaining indefinitely in our lives authority's influence, somewhat ironically as the Second World War is often framed as one *against* tyranny and the intrusion of authority into lives.

Interestingly enough leading up to the war, Germany's Weimar Republic possessed one of the most democratic constitutions in the world guaranteeing universal suffrage, proportional representation, and strong civil liberties. It was arguably more democratic than Britain's government, and notwithstanding Germany's reparation challenges was far more robust and stable than France's. Democracy failed in Germany when it became 'inconvenient' as hyperinflation took hold with the country's internal social contract faltering under the onerous terms dictated to it by the Treaty of Versailles. That treaty itself, as is the case with all contracts, expresses the idea of affiliation, alliance and contrast. Parties to a contract imply exclusion. As perimeters are defined so too is the sense of who belongs and who does not.

You will see a similar pattern develop *within* internal legislatures of modern democracies. For example looking at the composition of a Parliament, you'll typically see two or more hierarchies arranged below party leaders. These political parties have little formal recognition from a strictly constitutional perspective: with generally no reference made to them in foundational documents. Parliamentary 'whips'  maintain discipline and therefore cohesion so that parties develop an (institutional) awareness or intelligence and express a form of liveness with all its characteristics; a combination of anima and animus as they assert themselves with brand and persona (a coherent private key) which expresses a utility or value through affiliation to the domain. The domain in turn reflects back a perception of that identity a public key. The two are then reconciled through the mediating node. In a sense, public opinion polls and elections serve as that join in this particular context.
